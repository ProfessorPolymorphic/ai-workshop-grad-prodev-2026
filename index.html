Activity 1 · 25 min

Meet your tools

MindRouter → Vandal Chat

MindRouter: the engine

The University of Idaho’s own AI cluster. Vandal Chat runs on it.

60+ open-weight models

Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, gpt-oss, and more, from half a billion to over 100 billion parameters. The live list is on the status page.

Your data stays on campus

Every model runs on university-owned GPUs. No outside company sees your prompts.

More than chat

API keys for your own code, coding agents, transcription, and PDF-to-text. You can use the API in Activity 4.

Your turn · 8 min
  1. Open mindrouter.uidaho.edu. Watch the live status page for a moment: every request on campus flows through here.
  2. Log in with your University of Idaho account.
  3. Find the model list. Write down one small model (under 10B) and one large model (over 100B). You will use both in a minute.
  4. Already comfortable? Create an API key in your Dashboard and save it somewhere safe. You can use it in Activity 4.
Open MindRouter →

Vandal Chat: the front door

A chat window on MindRouter. Sign in with your university account.

Your turn · 12 min
  1. Compare two models. Ask your small model, then your large one: How many protein-coding genes are in the human genome? Give one number and one sentence of justification. Compare with your neighbor. Same answer? Which is right? How would you check?
  2. Check a source. Ask: List three peer-reviewed papers on [your thesis topic], with authors, year, and DOI. Pick one and paste its DOI into doi.org.
Open Vandal Chat →

Watch for

  • Different answers to the same question
  • A citation that looks perfect and does not exist
  • A real paper that does not say what the model claims

Report back

Did your DOI resolve? Right authors? Right claim? We will tally the room.

What the tools just showed you

Pick the model on purpose

Two models, two answers. Which model you used is a decision, and part of your record.

Know where your data goes

MindRouter and Vandal Chat keep it on campus. A commercial chatbot does not. Part 2 covers what still cannot go in.

Check against the source

A clean-looking answer is not a checked answer. The DOI, the paper, the data: go look.

Checkpoint Signed in to both MindRouter and Vandal Chat? If either one is not working, tell us now. Every activity today runs on these two tools.

Developing a prompt is a loop

Click a node in the flowchart to highlight the matching step.

  • Start with a broad, generic question.
  • Review the output for what you dislike.
    • Misusing terms? Add definitions.
    • Strangely formatted? Specify the output, and limit the options.
    • Guide its thinking with step-by-step instructions.
  • Change the prompt and repeat.

Tools we’ve built

Built at the University of Idaho.

MindRouter The university’s AI cluster: 60+ open-weight models on university GPUs, a web chat, and API keys for your own code and coding agents. (Guide: running agents) (GitHub)
Vandal Chat University AI chat, powered by MindRouter. Includes Deep Research: a team of agents that writes a cited report on one big question. (Deep Research)
Vandalizer Document intelligence from the AI4RA team. Pulls structured fields out of PDFs, chains tasks into workflows, and scores them against a hand-checked Golden Set using TaMPER. Built for research administration; limited early access; open source. (Manual) (GitHub)
